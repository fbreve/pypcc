## Fixed research question

What is the observed *current-production source* relative timing of **NumPy, Numba and Cython** in (1) complete `fit_predict`, (2) propagation only, (3) initialization/dispatch and (4) postprocessing, when the graph is prepared separately and cached per dataset? On the selected hosts, which preparation phases cost as much as or more than the fitted native kernels? Describe the **one-process sampled peak resident set size** separately and do not pretend it captures exact per-fit/temporary allocations.

This is intentionally a **new small master-only baseline**, not a performance optimization or repetition of the previously completed 3,600+3,600+432 historical fit campaigns. Historical binaries and source SHA differ from current master and must not be silently pooled.

## Interpretation / gate

This is a **descriptive pilot** on 3 datasets and GitHub-hosted machines. Repetitions share each fixed mask and one process and are not IID scientific samples. Windows/Linux run on different hosts. The existing Cython potential cache affects `early_stop=True`; because primary fixed-10k disables stopping, any performance finding is **not evidence against/for** that cache. Sampled RSS is a high-level memory diagnostic, not comparable to exact live peak of model states. The aim is to choose **a measurable production bottleneck** to investigate separately, in a future **single master-based optimization PR**, requiring exact same-backend array/RNG/stopping parity and native matched candidate-vs-control timing. **No production modification or merge is authorized from this baseline alone.**
